Navigating Market Challenges in 2027

Aadhya Associates · November 2026 · 3 min read

No one can predict exactly how the next year will unfold. What leaders can do is ask better questions about the forces most likely to affect their business, and prepare responses before they're needed. Here are the areas we encourage clients to think through as they plan for 2027.

1. Costs and margins

Input costs, wages, insurance and financing don't move in step, and pressure in one area can quietly erode margins even when sales look healthy.

  • Which of your costs have risen fastest over the past two years, and have your prices kept pace?
  • Which products, services or customers are least profitable once all costs are included?
  • If a major cost rose another 10%, what would you do first?

Knowing your margins by product and customer, not just in total, is the foundation for every other decision on this list.

2. Customer expectations

Customers, whether consumers or businesses, increasingly expect speed, transparency and convenience as standard. At the same time, many are more careful about where they spend.

  • How easy is it to buy from you, get an answer from you or resolve a problem with you?
  • What do customers say when they choose a competitor instead?
  • Are you clearly communicating the value you deliver, not just the price?

Small improvements in responsiveness and clarity often do more for loyalty than large investments elsewhere.

3. Technology and AI

Practical tools for automation, analytics and AI are now within reach of businesses of almost any size. The risk lies in two directions: investing in technology without a clear purpose, or ignoring changes that competitors use to serve customers faster and more cheaply.

  • Which repetitive tasks consume the most staff time today?
  • Where could better data improve your decisions on pricing, inventory or staffing?
  • Do you have the skills in-house to evaluate new tools, or do you need outside help?

Start with a specific business problem, then look for the simplest tool that solves it.

4. People and skills

Finding and keeping good people remains a challenge in many industries. As roles change with new technology, the skills a business needs are shifting too.

  • Which roles are hardest to fill, and what would happen if a key person left tomorrow?
  • Is critical knowledge documented, or does it live with a few individuals?
  • What are you doing to develop the people you already have?

5. Competition and positioning

New competitors can appear quickly, especially online, and established ones adapt. Businesses that are clear about who they serve and why they're different are better placed than those trying to be everything to everyone.

  • Can you explain in one sentence why customers should choose you?
  • Which customer segments are growing, and which are shrinking?
  • Are there adjacent markets or services where your strengths would carry over?

Turning questions into a plan

Answering these questions honestly usually reveals a short list of priorities: one or two risks to protect against and one or two opportunities to pursue. From there, a practical plan needs three things. It needs clear owners for each priority, a few simple measures to track progress, and a regular rhythm for reviewing and adjusting course.

Uncertainty isn't going away. Businesses that build the habit of asking these questions regularly, rather than once a year, will be the ones best placed to respond when markets shift.
